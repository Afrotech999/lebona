Crisis support

Emergency help is available 24/7

Lebona matching takes 24–48 hours. If you are in immediate danger, call your national helpline or go to the nearest hospital right now. Calls are free and confidential. If you can, take someone with you.

When you have a moment

Self-guided safety planning

Longer reading for getting through the next hour, supporting someone else, and planning ahead. Open whichever you need.

These are not a treatment. They are a way to get through the next hour, which is all you have to do.

1

Put distance between you and the means

Give the pills, the rope, the weapon or the keys to someone else, or lock them away. Distance buys time, and time is usually what turns this around.

2

Tell one person, in plain words

"I am thinking about killing myself and I don't want to be alone." A friend, a relative, a neighbour, or your national helpline. You are not a burden for saying it.

3

Agree not to act for the next 24 hours

Not forever. Just today. Suicidal intensity comes in waves and almost always drops.

4

Do one small physical thing

Cold water on your face and wrists. Walk outside. Eat something. It works on the body while the mind catches up.

5

Get to a hospital if the urge is rising

Emergency departments take this seriously and will not turn you away. Bring someone if you can. If you cannot travel, call an ambulance: Ethiopia 907 · Kenya 999 · Rwanda 912.

Ask directly

"Are you thinking about killing yourself?" Asking does not plant the idea. It gives permission to answer honestly, often for the first time.

Listen without fixing

Resist advice, silver linings and comparisons. "That sounds unbearable, tell me more" does far more work than any solution.

Do not promise secrecy

Say instead: "I will not tell people who don't need to know, but I am not going to keep a secret that could cost your life."

Stay, or hand over

Do not leave someone alone in an acute crisis. If you must go, hand them to a named person. Then follow up tomorrow, and the day after.

Look after yourself too

Supporting someone through this is heavy. You are allowed to get your own support. Our parents and carers circle exists for exactly this.

Know your limit

You are not their clinician. The goal is to get them to professional help, not to become the professional help.

A safety plan is one page that answers four questions in advance: what my warning signs are, what helps, who I call, and where I go. Written on a good day, it does the thinking for you on a bad one. Our therapists build one with every client who wants it.
